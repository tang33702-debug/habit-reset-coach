# Experiments, Review, and Optional Continuity

Use this file to turn advice into learning and to preserve continuity only with explicit permission.

## Minimum Viable Experiment

Define:

- **Protected outcome**: sleep, learning, work, health, relationship, money, or voluntary control.
- **Target behavior**: one observable action or stopping point.
- **Leading hypothesis**: what likely maintains the pattern and what observation would weaken it.
- **Primary change**: one main intervention the user can actually perform.
- **Duration**: a short period long enough to encounter several real opportunities.
- **Measures**: one to three low-burden indicators.
- **Prediction**: the intermediate and protected-outcome change expected if the hypothesis is useful.
- **Disconfirmation**: an observation that would count against the hypothesis.
- **Decision rule**: continue, adjust, replace, pause, or escalate based on the result.

Do not call an arbitrary number a scientific threshold. Explain that it is a temporary experiment rule chosen to learn or protect something.

In the user-facing proposal, state the hypothesis, temporary test, measure, and failure branch in compact language. Do not merely name a duration and call it an experiment. Example structure: “One possibility is X. For the next N real opportunities, change Y and observe Z. If Y is executed but Z does not change, stop treating X as the leading explanation and test A instead.”

## Match Measures to the Failure

- **Missing cue**: proportion of relevant opportunities in which the cue was noticed.
- **Cannot start**: start delay, number of starts at the cue, perceived resistance.
- **Cannot sustain**: engaged minutes, useful unit completed, interruption count, reason for stopping.
- **Cannot stop**: delay before entry, episode duration, stopping-cue success, downstream harm.
- **Lapse cascade**: time to recovery and whether the next protected block was preserved.
- **Goal mismatch**: willingness, perceived usefulness, and whether benefits actually matter to the user.

Never rely only on streak length. Avoid detailed counting when it increases shame, obsession, or compulsive checking. Do not turn measurement into a second burdensome habit.

## Review Decisions

- **Executed plus predicted change**: treat the hypothesis as provisionally supported, not proven; repeat or expand cautiously.
- **Executed without predicted change**: change the hypothesis or mechanism rather than blaming effort.
- **Not executed**: treat the intervention as currently unworkable; do not claim the maintaining hypothesis was supported or disproved.
- **Continue** when the protected outcome improves and the intervention is repeatable at acceptable cost.
- **Adjust** when the mechanism looks right but the dose, cue, timing, or difficulty is wrong.
- **Replace** when predicted intermediate effects do not occur or the intervention repeatedly cannot be executed.
- **Pause or drop** when the goal is not useful, owned, or timely.
- **Escalate** when lighter interventions fail despite correct targeting, or health and impairment signals exceed coaching scope.

Use disconfirming evidence. Example: if the book is visible and opened but the user still avoids it, “the cue was hidden” is no longer the leading explanation.

## Optional Habit Card

Create or update a continuity record only after explicit consent. Keep it brief:

```text
Protected outcome:
Current target:
Leading hypothesis:
Current experiment and review date:
Known helpful conditions:
Known failed or unacceptable interventions:
Escalation or safety notes requested by the user:
```

Do not record explicit sexual details, diagnoses, intimate disclosures, or broad personality judgments. Prefer “phone distance was not executable” over “lacks self-control.” Let the user inspect, correct, or delete the record. Do not imply that a Skill automatically remembers across tasks unless a record is actually available.
