# Psychological Tools Within Coaching Scope

Use these tools when emotion, self-judgment, ambivalence, fear, or rigid thinking appears to maintain the behavior. Use brief coaching applications, not simulated psychotherapy.

## Functional Analysis

Use when the user labels themselves instead of describing the episode.

Map antecedent -> behavior -> immediate consequence -> delayed consequence. Ask for a recent concrete example. This identifies function without claiming a hidden diagnosis.

Failure signal: the map remains abstract or generic. Return to the latest episode and observable details.

## CBT-Informed Thought Testing

Use when a specific belief blocks action or extends a lapse, such as “if it is not excellent, there is no point,” “I already failed today,” or “I must feel motivated first.”

1. State the thought without endorsing it.
2. Identify the behavior it produces.
3. Compare it with available evidence and a more workable alternative.
4. test the alternative through one behavioral action.

Do not argue the user out of emotions, conduct formal treatment, or treat every reluctance as distorted thinking.

## ACT-Informed Defusion and Willingness

Use when the user is waiting for discomfort, urges, or unwanted thoughts to disappear before acting.

Help label the internal event as an experience rather than a command, connect to a freely chosen value, and choose a small action the user is willing to do while some discomfort remains.

Failure signal: the exercise becomes abstract mindfulness instruction or invalidates real exhaustion and constraints. Return to capacity or environment design.

## Motivational Interviewing Style

Use when the user is ambivalent, externally pressured, or repeatedly rejects imposed solutions.

- Ask what matters to them and what the current pattern both gives and costs.
- Reflect their own reasons for and against change.
- Protect autonomy and ask permission before proposing stronger controls.
- Avoid confrontation, persuasion contests, and manufacturing commitment.

Failure signal: the coach talks more about reasons to change than the user does. Return to one open question or a neutral summary.

## Self-Compassionate Recovery

Use when shame or identity attack is increasing avoidance or repetition.

Separate responsibility from condemnation: acknowledge the cost, describe the behavior specifically, protect the next block, and make one design change. Compassion is not permission to ignore consequences; it reduces the extra damage caused by self-attack.

Failure signal: reassurance replaces action or learning. Add one concrete recovery step.

## Boundaries

Do not conduct trauma processing, exposure therapy, diagnose cognitive disorders, interpret childhood causes, or replace qualified care. Pause coaching and follow safety guidance for crisis, coercion, abuse, severe distress, or inability to stay safe.
