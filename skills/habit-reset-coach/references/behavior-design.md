# Behavior Design and Review

## Diagnose the Loop

Separate these common failure types:

- **Cannot start**: ambiguity, task size, fear of evaluation, low energy, or an easier reward is available.
- **Cannot continue**: difficulty mismatch, slow feedback, interruptions, or no finish line.
- **Cannot stop**: endless content, variable reward, avoidance relief, fatigue, loneliness, or arousal.
- **Only acts near deadlines**: deadlines may supply a salient cue, clearer structure, arousal, social consequence, or immediate relief after action.
- **Knows the intervention but cannot perform it**: the intervention may begin too late, be too large, remove a needed reward, or depend on planning and inhibition that are harder in the current state.

Treat these as hypotheses. Ask what was observable immediately before and after the behavior.

## Intervention Menu

Choose one primary lever:

- **Cue**: hide, relocate, schedule, or separate the cue from the context.
- **Friction**: test logging out, removing autoplay, grayscale, a wait step, charging outside reach, or required movement. These are candidates, not guaranteed fixes.
- **Default**: leave the desired file open, prepare materials, preselect the next lesson, or define the first visible action.
- **Scale**: reduce the action until it is realistically startable while preserving the user's option to decline; then grow only after repeated starts.
- **Replacement**: provide a short alternative for stimulation, relief, rest, connection, movement, or sexual tension.
- **Commitment**: use a real appointment, shared work session, public library, or trusted check-in. Never use humiliation or coercion.
- **Feedback**: make progress visible within the session rather than relying on distant rewards.

## Minimal Experiment

Define:

- **Target**: one observable behavior or protected outcome.
- **Hypothesis**: one sentence about what maintains the loop.
- **Intervention**: one main environmental or behavioral change.
- **Measures**: one to three measures such as start delay, total duration, bedtime, recovery time, completed learning unit, or perceived control.
- **Review**: a short period appropriate to the loop.
- **Escalation**: what evidence calls for another intervention or professional support.

Numbers chosen without evidence are temporary experiment thresholds. Explain what they are meant to learn or protect.

## Lapse Recovery

1. End or reduce the current episode when possible.
2. Protect the next important block: sleep, class, meal, work session, or relationship commitment.
3. Record one useful fact, not a confession: cue, state, location, first action, or failed barrier.
4. Restore the plan at the next opportunity; do not wait for tomorrow, Monday, or a new streak.
5. Change one design element before asking for more effort.

## Review Questions

- Did the behavior occur less automatically?
- Was there more delay or choice before it?
- Did duration, intensity, or downstream harm change?
- Did the user recover faster after a lapse?
- Was the intervention easy enough to repeat?
- What observation would justify continuing, adjusting, or stopping it?
